The problem

  • Teams can lose visibility across a busy shift.
  • Tasks may be passed verbally and become difficult to track.
  • Status information can become outdated quickly.
  • Operational tools can be too complicated for everyday staff use.

How it works

  • Staff enter through a simple role-based access flow.
  • A shift can be started and ended from the staff interface.
  • Authorised users can see live operational status.
  • Tasks move through assigned, acknowledged, skipped or completed states.

Why it is useful

  • Creates one shared operational picture.
  • Reduces repeated radio or verbal status checks.
  • Makes task ownership easier to understand.
  • Lets staff focus on the next useful action.
1. Start shiftThe user enters the staff experience and becomes available for the current shift.
2. Receive workAssigned tasks appear clearly with a visible status and response path.
3. Close the loopCompletion updates the operations view so authorised users can see the latest state.
Product status is intentionally described as a pilot. Production-grade permissions, privacy, reliability and background tracking still need to be proven before wider deployment.

The problem

When a site or service stops working, most users do not know whether to restart their device, reset Wi‑Fi, contact their provider or simply wait for the service to recover.

How it works

FixCheck uses signals a web app can realistically access, narrows the likely cause and converts the result into plain-language guidance.

Why it is useful

It aims to reduce confusion and unnecessary troubleshooting by telling the user what is most likely happening and what to try next.

1. Fast startNo forced account wall for the core diagnostic experience.
2. Clear analysisTechnical signals are translated into understandable findings.
3. Next actionThe result suggests a practical next step instead of dumping raw data.
A browser cannot inspect every part of a device or network. Where certainty is impossible, FixCheck describes the result as likely or inferred rather than guaranteed.

Product approach

Build, test, improve.

AI-assisted development helps move from idea to prototype quickly, but product quality still depends on the problem definition, user flow, testing and honest limitations.

01

Start with the problem

Define who needs the product, what they are trying to do and what a useful result looks like.

02

Prototype the real flow

Build small working stages and test the actual path a user takes instead of adding features for appearance.

03

Improve from evidence

Keep useful features, fix friction and be clear about what is still experimental.
